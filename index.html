Michael Blumhardt — Independent Research

My work examines statistical diagnostics, uncertainty auditing, and validation of scientific inference pipelines using public datasets and reproducible computational methods. Projects span astronomy, genomics, collider physics, and methods for evaluating AI-assisted research.

Current work

As of October 30, 2026, the managed portfolio’s two live projects are a black-hole X-ray binary spectroscopic outburst inventory and historical replay validation of research procedures. Both remain at prerequisite stages; this listing does not indicate that research execution has begun.

Deposits

  • GWAS annotation drift: Sensitivity of GWAS pathway interpretation to annotation version. The closed descriptive pilot’s outcome was INDETERMINATE-FINAL.
    Preprint posted (not peer reviewed): https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24548545
    Supplement: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24174310
  • Critterworld radius decomposition: A stationary Critterworld organism with a disabled controller could collect more food simply because its reach extended farther. Although mean encoded radius increased in all ten historical runs, the trend alone could not distinguish the opposing effects observed in replay.
    Preprint posted (not peer reviewed): https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24548891. Submitted for journal review.
    Artifact package: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22180749. Inspection and traceability package. Full historical replay remains unverified.
  • Preregistration and Analysis Code for a Study of Path Dependence in Mathlib Growth https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18475878. Project closed.

Methods

The work emphasizes explicit research questions, traceable inputs, executable analyses, and checks that connect scientific claims to the computations supporting them. Project records distinguish exploratory work, prerequisite preparation, completed analyses, and terminated studies.
