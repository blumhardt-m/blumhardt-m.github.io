Michael Blumhardt

Independent researcher
Statistical diagnostics and uncertainty auditing in large scientific datasets

Research Focus

Work focuses on identifying structural sources of error and uncertainty in large-scale scientific inference pipelines. Across domains, analyses examine how aggregated or compressed observables can appear stable while diverging when conditioned on latent variables tied to the data-generation process.

  • Validation and uncertainty diagnostics for large scientific data products
  • Detection of aggregation- and compression-induced degeneracy
  • Selection functions, completeness, and distribution shift
  • Cross-dataset and cross-domain consistency checks
  • Reanalysis of public datasets using reproducible pipelines

Selected Work

  • Non-identifiability of Nuisance Mechanisms in Reduced Collider Observables
    Shows that compressed collider observables can admit multiple indistinguishable nuisance explanations without conditional diagnostics, using CMS Open Data missing transverse momentum distributions.
    Under review, Journal of Instrumentation (JINST)
  • Background-Disjoint Cross-Validation Reveals Background-Dependent Errors in CNN Gravitational Lens Classifiers
    Demonstrates that conventional cross-validation can inflate reported performance due to background-dependent leakage, using gravitational lens classification as a case study.
    Under review, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society (MNRAS)
  • Gaia DR3 Quasar Parallax Systematics
    Identifies spatially structured parallax residuals in Gaia DR3 quasars and evaluates robustness under catalog selection and null controls.
    Under review, Astronomy & Astrophysics (A&A)
  • Annotation Version as a Hidden Degree of Freedom in GWAS Pathway Interpretation
    Quantifies how genome annotation evolution propagates through locus-to-gene mapping and pathway enrichment, producing threshold amplification effects.
    Under review, Bioinformatics

Methodological Approach

Analyses emphasize preregistered or protocol-driven workflows, falsifiable diagnostics, and conservative statistical inference. Projects are designed to remain informative under null outcomes and to expose structural limitations of inference procedures rather than optimize performance metrics.
